## Naming Pattern

Use Latin-like elemental taxonomy for registry names:

`<Element Root> <Ancestry Root>`

Examples:

- `Lumen Corvus`: light raven.
- `Ignis Canis`: fire canine.
- `Aqua Canis`: water canine.
- `Flora Cervus`: plant deer.
- `Fulgur Lepus`: electric hare.

Player-facing names can be warmer or brandable, but registry names should preserve the scientific/lab-catalog feeling.

## Evolution Pattern

Most lines should begin in baby or juvenile forms and grow into stronger elemental expressions over time.

Default line structure:

- Stage 1: baby or juvenile form, small and appealing, with subtle engineered elemental traits.
- Stage 2: adolescent or trained form, clearer combat role and stronger elemental biology.
- Stage 3: mature form, fully readable battle identity and premium art presence.

When creating elemental creature lines, be open to 2 or 3 evolution stages like classic creature RPGs. A line can stop at a strong second stage when that is the cleanest design, or reach a fully realized third stage when the creature is important enough to support it.

Not every species needs exactly three stages, but early starters, native biome creatures, and flagship species should usually be considered for this structure.

## Roster Expansion Direction

Grow the early roster toward roughly 20 distinct creature lines over time.

Long-term, build toward roughly 150 obtainable creatures or forms across the full game. Treat this as an obtainable roster target, not a requirement that every entry be a completely unrelated base animal.

The roster should not become only cute starter-like companions. Keep a wide emotional range:

- Cute and bondable.
- Strange and mysterious.
- Threatening and capable.
- Elegant and rare.
- Defensive and armored.
- Fast and predatory.
- Supportive but not harmless.

Even baby or juvenile forms should imply future danger or power through posture, eyes, anatomy, elemental organs, or behavior. Avoid making every creature feel like a puppy, plush toy, or mascot.

All animal-inspired Vivos must look threatening or full of dangerous potential, even as babies. They should not be smiling or read as harmless pets. A baby dog should already imply it will grow into a massive powerful guardian that needs guidance. A cat should suggest something uncanny or predatory, such as future panther, tiger, or another dangerous lineage.

## Biome Native Lines

New creatures should often be designed around native biomes. A creature should feel like it belongs somewhere specific, not like it was dropped into a generic encounter table.

When adding a creature line, define:

- Native biome.
- Encounter zone type.
- Elemental pressure or attunement.
- Why the player would seek it out.
- Whether it is common, rare, hidden, rescued, or gym-gated.
